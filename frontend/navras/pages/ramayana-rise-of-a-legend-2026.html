Ramayana: Rise of a Legend

Veera Adbhuta

We know where the story of Ramayana is going. Nitesh Tiwari’s smartest decision is to make us curious about how Ravana got there.

Story

Giving Ravana’s past more room brings parts of the epic that often stay in the background into focus. I kept coming back to the contrast between Rama and Ravana. One is bound by duty, the other is driven by vengeance, and the film gives you room to sit with both.

Direction

Tiwari’s choice reminded me of Christopher Nolan’s Dark Knight trilogy, where the villains are so compelling that they change how you see the hero’s choices. For me, that shift in perspective is what makes this retelling work.

The soundtrack plays with that contrast too. “Jai Jai Ram,” sung by Shreya Ghoshal and Arijit Singh, gives the album its devotional heart, while Vishal Dadlani’s “Ravana Tandav” brings a fiercer energy. A.R. Rahman composed both songs, and he shares scoring credit with Hans Zimmer.

Performances

Yash is the standout as Ravana. His body language does as much as his dialogue. The way he carries himself, holds a gaze, or simply stands still makes it hard to look anywhere else. You get the sense of a king who expects the room to fall silent when he walks in. That screen presence makes the time Tiwari spends on Ravana feel earned.

The rest of the cast gives the epic its familiar anchors. Ranbir Kapoor’s Rama brings duty and sacrifice into the story, while Sai Pallavi’s Sita gives those choices personal stakes. Ravie Dubey plays Lakshman, and Sunny Deol takes on Hanuman.

CineRaaga take

Watch it on the big screen with your family. For me, this is the most impressive big-screen telling of Ramayana to date. There’s something special about watching an epic that generations of Indian families have grown up hearing and coming out of the theatre wanting to talk about a character you thought you already knew.

CineRaaga Editorial
July 2026

Dhamaal 4 is loud, noisy, cheerful, and instantly forgettable. It plays like background entertainment — the kind of film that works best while you're folding clothes, eating dinner, or scrolling your phone. Sit down and give it your full attention, and you'll notice how thin everything actually is.

Script

Written by Paritosh Painter, Balvinder Singh Suri, Bunty Rathore, and Ved Prakash — with Bunty Rathore handling the dialogues — the script exists mainly to push the ensemble from one chaotic set-piece to the next. There's no story you'll remember on the drive home, no dialogue worth repeating. It's a treasure-hunt frame stretched over 143 minutes without ever earning it.

Direction

Indra Kumar returns to the franchise he built and hits every beat you'd expect — loud action, broad slapstick, ensemble chaos. What's missing is any attempt to sharpen the formula. Everything is loud where it could have been funny; noise substitutes for wit throughout.

Acting

Ajay Devgn, Arshad Warsi, Riteish Deshmukh, and Jaaved Jaaferi still have easy chemistry, and you can feel them enjoying being back together. Individually though, no one is stretched — the film asks them to shout, mug, and react, and they oblige. Sanjay Mishra gets the occasional real laugh; Ravi Kishan is wasted as the villain.

Music

The music is average, nothing new — a few functional tracks that pass the time without leaving much of a mark.

CineRaaga says

Dhamaal 4 is comfort junk food — not memorable, not clever, not offensive. Skip it in the theatres and wait for OTT. If you liked the earlier Dhamaal films for exactly what they were, this one delivers more of that. If you were hoping the fourth installment might raise its game, it doesn't.
